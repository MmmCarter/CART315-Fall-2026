Date: October 31, 2026

In this walking simulator, I chose to create a subway station as the setting and named it "Always a Step Late". I wanted to explore the feeling of arriving just too late, as well as the possibility of letting go afterward. Players pass by two platforms, hearing the sound of a subway train passing by just before reaching each one, and eventually leaves the station for an outdoor plaza in the early morning. I hope to hint at an experience through the environment and sound, allowing players to interpret it on their own, rather than explaining the story through dialogue.

I built most of the environment using Unity’s basic cubes.Repeating walls, ceilings, and platforms helped establish the station’s structure without requiring many external models. The two similar platforms also supported the idea of repetition: after missing the first metro, the player enters another space that seems to offer the same opportunity. However, this approach also has its limitations. Repetition can convey a sense of frustration, but it also risks making the visual experience monotonous. If I developed the project further, I would introduce more subtle differences between the two platforms while maintaining their overall similarity, or add a train model and have it actually depart.

Sound became an important part of the experience. I used a recording of a real subway train passing by, triggering the sound near the platform so that I could imply its movement without having to create a full subway model and animation. Footsteps reinforced the connection between the player’s walking and the open space. Upon reaching the exit, soft music gradually begins to play and loops continuously. I used this change to mark a shift from anticipation toward calm.

Lighting supported the same transition. Indoors, I used cool-toned lighting and glowing fluorescent tube surfaces to create a closed, artificially lit atmosphere. Outdoors, this contrasts with the blue morning sky and warm sunlight. I also added a bench to provide a visual focal point for the plaza and suggest that this is a place to linger. My intention was for reaching the outdoors to feel like an ending, even without a completion message.

The most challenging work involved making the space feel continuous. Gaps between the wider platforms and narrower corridors exposed the sky and broke the illusion of an enclosed station. Adding closure walls and an exterior shell helped address this. I also encountered unwanted illumination, which made me distinguish between an actual opening and a lighting or shadow problem.

This project helped me understand how walking, repetition, sound, and changes in light can carry an experience with very few actions. My next priority would be to have other people play it without explanation. I would want to learn whether they understand the missed-metro idea, whether the repeated spaces remain interesting, and whether the outdoor ending communicates the relief I intended.
