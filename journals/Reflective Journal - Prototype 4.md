Date: November 16, 2026

In this iteration, I wanted to give players more ways to interact with the environment while preserving the original intention of the walking simulator. The previous prototype centered primarily on repeatedly missing the metro and eventually finding a moment of peace outdoors. My goal is to make this emotional progression more interactive.

The first enhancement was the addition of a service inquiry button at each of the two platforms. Previously, players could only hear the metro pass by and continue walking. Now, the button allows players to actively seek a response from the station. The display at the first platform asks the player to wait, then informs them that service has ended; the display at the second platform gives that answer immediately. I wanted this difference to suggest that continuing to wait might no longer be useful. The interaction offers a response without changing the outcome. I also added a short beep that plays when the button is pressed. Later presses still produce audio feedback, but they do not restart the service request. This separates acknowledging the player’s action from repeating the whole event.

The second enhancement was the ability to sit on the outdoor bench. The bench had originally been a visual suggestion of rest; making it usable allowed the player to choose to pause. Sitting temporarily disables walking while allowing the player to look around. Standing restores the previous position and controls. I then made further adjustments to the transition between standing and sitting. In the initial version, the camera angle would switch instantly, which felt a bit abrupt for an interaction intended to show a sense of calm. I replaced the abrupt switch with a brief, smooth transition with fade-in and fade-out effects, and disabled repeated input during the transition. It made me pay more attention to pacing at the scale of a single action. Even a small camera movement can affect whether an interaction feels restful or mechanical.

There are still questions I would like to investigate through play testing. The buttons suggest that players can take action, but the fixed result could feel frustrating if players expect to summon a metro. The next step is to verify whether these interactions express the shift from expectation to acceptance that I intend to show, rather than just simply confirming the functions.
